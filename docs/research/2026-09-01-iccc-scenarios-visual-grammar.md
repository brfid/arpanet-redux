# ICCC scenarios visual grammar for the passive NCC desk

- **Observed:** 2026-09-01
- **Primary source:** SRI Network Information Center, [*Scenarios for Using the ARPANET at the International Conference on Computer Communication*](https://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/access/text/2019/07/102784024-05-001-acc.pdf), NIC 11863, October 24-26, 1972, Computer History Museum collection 102784024
- **Scope:** Presentation grammar only; no change to evidence authority, typed contracts, reducers, retained artifacts, or simulator control

## Question

Can the completed coexistence desk look native to the documented 1972 ARPANET demonstration without copying the historical scan, pretending that the scenario booklet was an NCC operator display, or weakening the modern evidence distinctions the desk must explain?

## Source review

The complete 59-page scan was reviewed as a contact sheet, then the cover, introductory statement, scenario conventions, table of contents, representative ITS procedure pages, survey tables, annotation columns, and a character-built mathematical diagram were inspected at higher resolution. The cover uses an ochre stock, centered sans-serif hierarchy, a small document identity in the corner, and visible comb binding. Interior pages use an off-white sheet, generous margins, monospaced typescript, centered section titles, numbered procedures, underlined user input, boxed control-key abbreviations, italic explanatory comments, dotted leaders, low-ink tables, and character-built diagrams.

Those devices are functional rather than ornamental. Numbering defines the procedure, underlining separates operator input from computer output, boxes identify nonprinting keys, italic text explains likely trouble spots, and dotted leaders connect an index label to its page or category. The booklet therefore supports a manual-like interaction model more strongly than a decorative “retro computer” treatment.

The booklet is a participant scenario manual, not evidence of the NCC's historical lightbox, alarm, hard-copy log, or summary-terminal layout. The separate [NCC telemetry research](2026-08-30-ncc-telemetry.md) remains authoritative for those operator concepts. The adaptation combines the scenario booklet's documentary presentation grammar with the desk's already-validated NCC evidence model; it does not claim that this browser view reproduces a historical NCC screen.

## Adaptation

The viewer becomes one project-authored comb-bound observation folio. An ochre cover establishes the scenario identity and completed composition verdict; the interior uses paper, toner, graphite, muted observation ink, ochre verdict ink, and red-pencil exception ink. A condensed sans-serif is limited to the cover while monospaced text carries procedures, evidence, tables, and controls.

The horizontal evidence sequence remains because it expresses a real ordered relationship that the booklet does not need to represent. It is restyled as a scenario register with boxed markers and typed annotations rather than replaced by a historically unsupported shared clock. Independent application, journey, and line conclusions become numbered result steps. The fixed topology becomes a low-ink diagram; configured links remain dashed, the accepted reciprocal pair retains a separate ochre box, and later endpoint classifications remain distinct marks. Journey boundaries become manual-like keys with underlined states, and authority explanations become italic annotations beside the facts they qualify.

The comb binding is the one deliberate visual signature. No fake stains, torn paper, raster grain, photographed binding, scanned typography, or copied page image is embedded. Responsive behavior retains the folio edge while allowing every wide evidence structure to scroll independently, and keyboard focus remains visible without relying on color alone.
